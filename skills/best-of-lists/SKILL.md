# Get onto local "best of" lists

Lists and articles are the single biggest source AI assistants cite for local recommendations. Being on the right 5 lists can matter more than another 50 directory listings.

## Inputs to ask for
- Business name, service, city or neighborhood, website.
- What genuinely sets the business apart (specialty, hours, price, owner story, awards). Ask for facts, not slogans.

## Steps
1. **Find the lists.** Search Google and Bing for:
   - "best [service] in [city]"
   - "top [service] [city] [current year]"
   - "[service] [neighborhood] guide"
   - "[city] small business spotlight", "new businesses in [city]"
   Also check local news sites, city magazines, neighborhood blogs, chamber of commerce pages and community newsletters.
2. **Qualify each result.** Keep pages that are recent (updated in the last 1 to 2 years), are about the right city, and appear to be edited by a person. Skip pay-to-play award sites and pages that only rank paid listings, unless the owner chooses to pay.
3. **Find the contact.** For each page, find the author or editor, or a "suggest a business" form, from the page or the site's contact or about page.
4. **Draft one pitch per page.** Keep it under 120 words. Each pitch should:
   - Say which list or article it's about.
   - Give 2 or 3 concrete facts that fit that list's angle.
   - Offer something useful (photos, an interview, a quote, a tasting or trial visit).
   - Sound like a person. No templates with obvious merge fields.

## Output
A table: Page | Site type | Last updated | Contact or form | Fit (high/med/low). Then the drafted pitches for the high-fit pages.

## Rules
- The owner sends the pitches themselves. Never send email on their behalf without explicit approval.
- No paying for placement unless the owner decides to, and then it must be disclosed as sponsored.
- No fake reviews, fake "customer" comments, or sock-puppet suggestions.
